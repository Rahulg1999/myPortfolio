Sheet 02  ·  Practice

A —

Offline is the default

The network is treated as a bonus. Local database as the source of truth, a background sync engine that survives the app being killed, and conflicts resolved without losing an operative's afternoon of work.

  • Drift · sqflite · ObjectBox
  • WorkManager & foreground services
  • Conflict-aware sync queues
  • Unsynced counts on the dashboard

B —

Evidence, not vibes

Compliance work needs a record of who said what, where, and when. Pin-level photo evidence on drawings, on-image markup, captured signatures, dynamic forms driven by a rule engine, PDF reports read in the app.

  • Pin placement on scanned drawings
  • Signatures & re-signing intervals
  • Geofencing · biometric auth · QR
  • MFA / OTP, multi-tenant access

C —

AI where it pays rent

Gemini put to work on real internal problems: an assistant inside an API-monitoring tool that answers questions and drafts test cases and documentation, plus a dozen focused tools built on the same API.

  • Google Gemini API integration
  • Prompt engineering for repeatable output
  • Document Q&A, image analysis
  • Chrome MV3 automation extensions

FireArrest

Flutter · Offline-first fire-safety compliance

v10.0.0 · IN SERVICE

One of the team's largest mobile codebases, and the one I led. Inspectors work inside buildings with no reception: they drop pins on drawing images, attach photo evidence with on-image markup, complete dynamic forms driven by a rule engine, scan asset QR codes and sign method statements on screen. I built the background sync engine on WorkManager and foreground services so all of it uploads reliably — with conflict handling — the moment a signal returns. W-04

HRM OTM

Flutter · Multi-tenant HR & leave

SHIPPED

A multi-tenant HR app running as a two-client, multi-flavor build from one codebase, with separate application IDs and signing per client. I contributed leave and balance tracking, clock-in/out and reporting, and OAuth2 login that injects the tenant header from the cached company record. I also built the face-attendance path: Face attendance runs entirely on the handset — MobileFaceNet embeddings and a MiniFASNetV2-SE anti-spoof classifier, so no face image leaves the phone, with a session policy that coaches framing and head tilt instead of failing people, covered by unit tests.
